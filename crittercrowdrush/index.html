This Privacy Policy explains what information the Critter Crowd Rush mobile game ("the app") stores on Android devices and iPhone, what information Google may receive as our advertising and game statistics partner, and the choices you have. Where something works differently on Android and on iPhone, this policy says so. "We", "us" and "our" mean Cae's Games, the developer of the app.

At a glance

  • No account, no sign-up form, no chat and no user-generated content. The game never asks for your name, email address or phone number.
  • Your level progress and settings are saved only on your device. We have no server of our own, and your saved game is never sent to us.
  • The game has no account and no cloud save, and if we ever add either, we will update this page before that version of the game is released.
  • The game is free and has no in-app purchases. It is funded by ads.
  • The app shows ads through Google AdMob. Google, and advertising companies that Google works with, may collect information such as your device's advertising ID, IP address and ad interactions in order to show and measure ads. On iPhone, Google only gets Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) if you allow tracking when the app asks.
  • The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase to count, for example, how many players come back and how far they get (see section 5). It sends game events with an ID for this installation of the app, never your name or email address. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, it does so only with your consent.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent first and lets you change your choice later with the Privacy Choices link on the home screen. In US states with privacy laws, you can opt out with the same link (see section 6 and the US state privacy notice). On iPhone, you can also turn tracking off at any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
  • The app is not directed at children under 13.

1. Who we are

Critter Crowd Rush is developed and published by Cae's Games, an independent developer. We are responsible for this app and for this Privacy Policy. If you have a question or request about privacy, email us at caesgames.dev@gmail.com.

2. Data stored on your device

To save your game, the app stores a small amount of information locally on your device using the Unity engine's standard local storage (PlayerPrefs):

  • your level progress (the highest level you have unlocked)
  • your sound effects and vibration on/off settings
  • whether you have already steered your crowd, so the short "Drag to steer" tip isn't shown again
  • for the game statistics (see section 5): which first-time milestones (level 1, 5, 10 or 20) the game has reported or still has to report, and whether statistics are paused or off because consent is missing

Google's software in the app, and the Unity engine the game is built with, also keep some technical information on your device, such as your ad consent choice (see section 6), and Google Analytics' ID for this installation of the app, the time the app was first opened and events waiting to be sent (see section 5). Apart from the game statistics, which go only to Google Analytics and are processed there for us (section 5), this information is not sent to us or to any server of our own. On iPhone, your answer to Apple's tracking question is kept by iOS itself (see section 6).

We can't see your saved game, and it is never sent to us or to Google. It stays on your device until you clear the app's data or uninstall (delete) the app. Because there is no account or cloud save, we can't restore it for you. If your device's own backup is turned on (Android's backup with Google, or iCloud Backup or a computer backup on iPhone), this data may be included in your personal device backup. That backup is controlled by your Google or Apple account settings, and we can't access it.

The game itself can be played offline. An internet connection is used only to check whether a privacy message is needed where you are, to load and show ads, and to send game statistics (see sections 4, 5 and 6).

3. What we do not collect

  • The game has no account and no sign-up form. It never asks for your name, email address, phone number, photos, contacts or any other personal details.
  • There's no chat, no social features and no user-generated content.
  • The app does not ask for permission to use your device's location (GPS). Google may estimate a general location from your IP address, as described in sections 4 and 5.
  • The game has no in-app purchases, so it never handles payments or payment details.
  • We don't use crash-reporting tools, and the game contains no Google Play Games software. The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase for game statistics, as described in section 5. Unity's analytics and crash-reporting services are switched off in the game. Google's ads SDK brings a few small Google support libraries of its own, which are part of the ads software described in section 4. The Unity engine the game is built with may send Unity Technologies basic technical information about your device, such as its model and operating system version, under Unity's privacy policy.
  • We don't run any servers of our own. Your saved game is never uploaded; only the game statistics described in section 5 are sent, to Google Analytics. Only your device's own backup may include your saved game (see section 2).
  • We don't sell your personal information for money. US state privacy laws define a "sale" and "sharing" broadly, though, and letting Google's ads software collect data for ads may count as either one. See section 12 to opt out.

4. Advertising by Google AdMob

Critter Crowd Rush is free to play and is funded by ads. Ads are provided by Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK, which is built into the app. The app shows two kinds of ads:

  • Rewarded ads (optional): when you lose a level, you can choose to watch a video to skip to the next level (the Skip Level button, which says "Watch an ad" under it). The level is skipped only if you watch the video to the end. Watching is always your choice. If no video is available at that moment, the button doesn't appear, and you can simply tap Try Again.
  • Interstitial ads: after you complete level 3 and later levels, a full-screen ad may appear when you tap Next Level. It never appears in the first 60 seconds after the app starts, or within 60 seconds of the previous full-screen ad, including a video you chose to watch.

We ask Google to show only ads rated G ("general audiences", suitable for all ages). Ads rated for parental guidance (PG), for teens (T) or for mature audiences (MA) are blocked.

So that an ad is ready when it is needed, the app loads ads in the background soon after it starts, as soon as Google's consent tool allows ads to be requested (see section 6). Each time the app starts, Google's consent tool also checks with Google whether a privacy message is needed where you are. This means Google can receive the information below even in a session in which you don't see an ad.

5. Game statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To learn how the game is played, for example how many new players come back the next day, which levels are too hard, and whether people who find the game through our ads really play it, the app uses Google Analytics for Firebase, a service from Google. The app sends Google:

  • Game events: when a run starts and whether it was won or lost (the level number); when you pass level 1, 5, 10 or 20 for the first time, by winning it or by skipping it with a video; and when a rewarded video gives you its reward. The game has no names, email addresses or accounts, so none are sent.
  • Events Google's software records by itself: the first time the app is opened, when it is opened or updated, how long it is used, which screens are shown, operating system updates, ads that are requested, shown, tapped or rewarded (reported by Google's ads software in the app), and, on Android, when the app's data is cleared or the app is removed.
  • Identifiers: an app instance ID that Google Analytics creates for this installation of the app; on Android, the Android advertising ID; on iPhone, the IDFV and, only if you allow tracking when the app asks, the IDFA.
  • Device and app information: device model, operating system version, language, app version, and your general location (country or region), which Google estimates from your IP address. On Android, Google Analytics also asks Google Play which link or ad the game was installed from (the "install referrer").

What we use it for: we see this information only as statistics in our Google Analytics account, such as "4 in 10 new players came back the next day". We use it to improve the game and to measure and improve our own ads for Critter Crowd Rush: the events "first open", "level 1 passed for the first time" and "level 5 reached" may be shared with our Google Ads account, so that Google can show our ads to people who are likely to enjoy the game. The app tells Google Analytics that this data must not be used for personalised advertising. We don't use it to build advertising audiences, we can't link it to your name or email address, and we don't sell it.

On iPhone, the app lets Google use these statistics for measuring our ads only if you choose Allow when Apple asks about tracking (see section 6). If you choose Ask App Not to Track, they are used only for our own statistics.

Your choice: Google Analytics starts switched off when the app is installed. Each time the app starts, Google's consent tool first checks whether a privacy message is needed where you are (see section 6). In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, game statistics are only collected if you agree, in the consent form, to storing or accessing information on your device. Until you have answered, Google Analytics stays off and sends nothing. If you don't agree, it stays off and the app deletes the statistics data stored on your device. Elsewhere, game statistics are switched on right after that check. Before Google Analytics is switched on, Google's Firebase software only notes on your device when the app was first opened, so that this first opening can be counted if statistics are switched on later. On iPhone, it may also register this installation of the app with Firebase, without sending any game statistics. You can change your choice at any time with the Privacy Choices link on the home screen (see section 6). Uninstalling the app resets the app instance ID, and you can reset or delete your advertising ID or turn off tracking as described in section 6.

Google's role: Google processes this information for us under the Google Analytics terms and the Google Privacy Policy (see also how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services). Event data linked to an app instance ID is kept for 14 months and then deleted. All of it is sent over an encrypted connection.

6. Consent and privacy settings

The app uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) to ask for your consent in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom and Switzerland. If consent is required, a consent form appears when you first open the app, before any ads are requested. If your device is offline the first time, the form appears later, when the app can reach Google and you are not in the middle of a run. The form explains how Google and its ad partners use data and lets you accept or decline. Your answer about storing or accessing information on your device also decides whether the app collects game statistics (see section 5). In US states with privacy laws, the same tool lets you opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of your personal information for ads (see section 12).

Changing your choice: if you are in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland or a US state whose privacy law Google's tool supports, the game shows a Privacy Choices link at the bottom of the home screen, next to the Privacy Policy link. Tap it to reopen Google's privacy form and change your choice at any time. Changing your choice affects future ad requests and, in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, game statistics. Google's tool saves your choice on your device, so you aren't asked again every time you play.

Tracking permission on iPhone: Apple requires apps to ask for your permission before they, or partners such as Google, track you across apps and websites owned by other companies, for example by using the IDFA for ads. The app may ask you this through Google's consent tool: you then see a short explanation screen, shown by Google's tool, followed by Apple's own question. Choose Allow to let the app's ads use the IDFA (and Google use the game statistics for measuring our ads, see section 5), or Ask App Not to Track to keep it from them. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, or if the app never asks, the IDFA is not used. Ads still appear either way, and the whole game works the same.

You can also control ads and tracking on your device:

  • Android: delete or reset your advertising ID. Open your device's Settings, then Privacy (or Google), then Ads. The exact menu names depend on your device and Android version. After you delete the advertising ID, apps can no longer use it to show you personalised ads.
  • iPhone: turn off tracking. Open Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Tracking. Turn off the switch next to Critter Crowd Rush (it appears there once the app has asked) to stop the app from using the IDFA, or turn off Allow Apps to Request to Track so that no app can ask you.
  • Uninstalling the app stops all data collection by the app and its SDKs. On iPhone, deleting every app from us also resets the IDFV.

7. Personalised and non-personalised ads

  • Personalised ads are chosen based on information such as your advertising ID and your activity in other apps and on other websites. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google only shows personalised ads if you give consent through the consent form. On iPhone, the IDFA is only used for this if you have also allowed tracking (see section 6).
  • Non-personalised ads aren't based on your past behaviour. They are chosen using the context, such as the app you are using and your general location (for example, the country or city estimated from your IP address). Even for non-personalised ads, Google may still use cookies or device identifiers, where permitted, to limit how often an ad is shown, to produce aggregated ad reports and to fight fraud and abuse.
  • If you don't consent to the use of your device for storing or accessing information, Google may show limited ads that use even less data, or no ads at all. The game can always be played either way.

8. Device permissions

The app does not ask for access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or files. On Android, it declares these permissions, plus one internal permission that only parts of the app itself can use. None of them shows you a permission prompt:

  • Internet and network state: to check whether a privacy message is needed, to load and show ads, and to send game statistics.
  • Vibration: for short vibrations during play and when you tap buttons. You can turn vibration off with the vibration button on the home screen.
  • Advertising ID and Android ad services: added by Google's ads SDK so it can use the Android advertising ID and, on devices that support them, Android's privacy-preserving ad APIs (such as Topics and Attribution Reporting), under your device's ad privacy settings (see section 6).
  • Install referrer (Google Play): added by Google Analytics for Firebase so it can ask Google Play which link or ad the game was installed from (see section 5).
  • Wake lock and foreground service: added by libraries that come with Google's ads and analytics software (Android's WorkManager and Google measurement libraries), so they can run short background tasks, such as sending game statistics. The game itself doesn't run any background service.

On iPhone, the only permission the app may ask for is the tracking permission described in section 6.

9. Legal bases (EEA and UK users)

Where the GDPR or the UK GDPR applies, we rely on:

  • Performance of our contract with you to provide the game and to answer your support requests.
  • Consent for personalised ads and for storing or accessing information on your device for advertising, for game statistics with Google Analytics (section 5), and on iPhone for the use of the IDFA (Apple's tracking permission). You can withdraw consent at any time with the Privacy Choices link on the home screen (section 6), or on iPhone in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking. Withdrawal doesn't affect processing that took place before.
  • Legitimate interests for showing limited ads that fund the free game, for choosing and measuring ads without a personal profile, for preventing fraud and abuse and keeping the app secure, and for checking with Google, each time the app starts, whether a privacy message is needed where you are. Non-personalised ads also need your consent to store or access information on your device; without it, Google shows only limited ads (section 7). You can object at any time by emailing us. For ad data, you can also use the Privacy Choices link (section 6).

The data saved on your device stays on your device and is used only so the game works as you would expect.

Do you have to give us data? No. You can play without giving us any personal data. The data Google's ads software collects is needed to show ads, but if you decline consent, the game still works the same, and in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland no game statistics are collected.

Profiling: personalised ads use profiling by Google, based on your advertising ID and your activity in other apps and on websites. We don't make any decisions about you based only on automated processing. You can object to profiling for ads at any time with the Privacy Choices link, where it is shown (section 6), or with your device's ad settings.

10. Children

Critter Crowd Rush is not directed at children under the age of 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The app doesn't ask for your age and has no special features for children. The game has no sign-in, no account and no purchases.

If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child under 13 has used the app, please contact us at caesgames.dev@gmail.com. We hold no account or game data for your child, because the game keeps its data on the device. The game statistics in Google Analytics (section 5) are linked only to a random ID for an installation of the app, so we can't find them by name. We will answer your questions and explain how to limit or remove data held by Google, for example by deleting the device's advertising ID (on iPhone, by turning off tracking) and uninstalling the app.

11. Data retention

  • On your device: your game data and settings stay on your device until you clear the app's data or uninstall the app.
  • Google AdMob: Google keeps the information it collects for the periods described in its own policies. See https://policies.google.com/privacy and How Google retains data.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase: event data linked to an app instance ID is kept for 14 months, then deleted. Reports that only show totals, such as the number of players per day, may be kept longer.
  • Emails you send us: if you contact us, we keep your message and email address only as long as we need to answer you and handle your request, and then delete them, unless the law requires us to keep them longer.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to:

  • EEA, UK and Switzerland (GDPR, UK GDPR, Swiss FADP): access your personal data, have it corrected or deleted, restrict or object to processing, receive a copy in a portable format, and withdraw consent at any time. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.
  • California and other US states with privacy laws (such as the CCPA/CPRA): know what personal information is collected, ask for it to be deleted or corrected, and opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising. We don't sell personal information for money. Personalised advertising based on your advertising ID may count as "sharing" under these laws. How to opt out: in states where the law gives you this right, the game shows a Privacy Choices link at the bottom of the home screen (see section 6). Tap it to open Google's privacy message and opt out. Google's ads SDK then restricts how your information is used for ads in this app. You can also use your device's own controls, such as deleting or resetting your Android advertising ID, or turning off tracking on iPhone (see section 6). If you email us, we will help you and explain these steps. We can't opt you out of ad data use ourselves, because we don't receive your advertising ID or other ad data. We won't discriminate against you for using these rights.

13. International transfers

Google, including its ad and analytics services, may process information in the United States and other countries where it or its partners operate, so the information described in sections 4 and 5 may leave the country you are in. For data from the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google LLC is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, its UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and it also uses the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. See Google's data transfer frameworks. If you email us, your message is stored by our email provider, Google (Gmail), and read by us in the country where we are based, which may be outside your country.

14. Security

The data the app saves on your device is protected by your device's app sandbox (on Android and on iOS), which stops other apps from reading it. According to Google, data collected by the Google Mobile Ads SDK is encrypted in transit using TLS, and Google Analytics for Firebase also sends its data over an encrypted connection. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, but we only keep the data the game needs.
